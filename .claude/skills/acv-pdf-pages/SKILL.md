# ACV PDF page map

`data/manual/pdf-pages.json` tells the site, per cote, which PDF pages hold each part of the register and which register page sits on which PDF page. Work register by register: **plan → profile → read pages → write the entry → check → record doubts**. A register is done when its entry passes `scripts/check.sh`, every register part has its first and last page read on the image, and every reading not made on the image is listed in `data/manual/pdf-pages-a-verifier.md`.

## Steps

1. **Plan.** `uv run --with numbers-parser scraper/pdf_plan.py --match "^E[bd] 92/" --hints index_paroisses.numbers` lists the registers without an entry. For each: the inventory parts and their `pp.`, the répertoire lines (séparé, incorporé, sans), the note, the saved profile, and the user's hand-made « Contenu PDF » ranges (PDF ranges only, often 1-2 pages off). Without `--hints` it runs with plain `python3`. In a worktree the `.numbers` file stays in the main checkout: `--hints ../../../index_paroisses.numbers`.
   - Screenshots fill the context faster than anything else: Rougemont's 21 registers, four of them Ed volumes whose offset drifts all along, used about half of it. When the first anchors show drift in more than about three Ed volumes, map the Eb and regular Ed registers in one session and the drifting Ed volumes in another.
   - Triage the Ed volumes right after the profile step, before mapping any: one `numbers()` call per volume on its first register page, two pages in the middle and its last register page. Volumes whose offset differs between these reads drift; decide then which go to a second session. Lutry found its six drifting Ed volumes only after mapping half the parish.
2. **Profile.** Open `https://davel-vd.ch/` with `preview_start`, inject the full text of `scraper/davel_pdf.js` with `javascript_tool` (when the local file has no unpushed changes, `(0, eval)(await (await fetch('https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nyg/acv-genealogie-index/<commit>/scraper/davel_pdf.js')).text())` injects it without pasting it), run the `pdf.enqueue({...})` call printed by the plan, in its own `javascript_tool` call, then `await pdf.idle(30000)` in the next ones until `queue` is empty (the default 45 s wait reaches the tool's own time limit, and the call fails), then `pdf.exportProfiles()`: the tool saves the padded result to a file; `python3 scraper/save_export.py <file> pdf-profiles`. About 3-5 requests per Eb/Ed PDF; the whole PDF is never downloaded.
3. **Read the profile.** `strips` are the narrow colour-chart scans the operator put before each physical volume: a strip after page 1 starts a separate répertoire volume (or a second scanning batch). `segments` group pages by format: separate répertoires are another format than the register. `text` is false on every PDF seen so far: no OCR.
4. **Read pages.** `await pdf.numbers(path, [6, 7, 106, 206, 285, 286])` draws one row per page holding its top-left and top-right corners side by side, where versos and rectos carry their number; screenshot it. Up to 12 pages per call: the rows shrink to fit the pane, so 12 Eb page numbers read in one screenshot. `await pdf.view(path, [6, 285], { band: 0.06, from: 0.02 })` draws a full-width band of each page, for headings and dates; `left`/`right` (0-1) crop it, and the result's `hidden` lists the bands that did not fit the pane. `pdf.view(path, [58, 59, 60, 61])` shows whole pages side by side to recognise covers, title pages, blank leaves and répertoires: at most 8, more are too small to tell apart. To find where a register ends, look for the marbled divider or the first répertoire page in a few whole pages at a stride, then bisect. One ~1 MB request per new page, cached afterwards. For 2-5 pages, `view` with `band`, `from` and `left`/`right` reads better than `numbers`, whose rows grow too tall and miss the number. Screenshot again when a band shows blank: the pane can lag. `pdf.close()` removes the overlay.
   - Settings that read: Eb pages `numbers(…, { from: 0.01, band: 0.05 })`; Ed printed forms (« Page N », « Folio N ») `{ from: 0.055, band: 0.045, width: 0.4 }`, the number sitting nearer the middle, down to `from: 0.09` when a year is written above the heading; plain-paper Ed registers like Eb, or `{ from: 0.025, band: 0.045, width: 0.25 }` (Ed 78/8-14). A binding photographed at the top of the page pushes the number down (Eb 78/6: `from: 0.075`). Adjust `from` until the number shows, `width` (default 0.3) to widen the corners. Turned pages take `rotate: true`.
   - For each register part, read its first and last page: the number written on the page and its PDF page. When the offset differs between the two ends, bisect with bands until you find where it changes. When the two ends agree, still read one page every 100 PDF pages in between: a photographed-twice page and a skipped number cancel out at the ends. Read one every 50 PDF pages in annonces volumes: Lutry's Ed 78/8-14 each looked regular at the ends and hid one bis page, a run of skipped numbers, a repeated pair of numbers or an unnumbered page.
   - Read one page of each répertoire: its references name the pagination the entry must use.
5. **Write the entry** in a scratch JSON file as each register is read, then merge it with `python3 scraper/add_pdf_pages.py <scratch.json>` (`--replace` to rewrite cotes already there): it checks every entry as the build does, compares `pdfPages` with the profile, and keeps the file's layout. Parts go in PDF order:
   ```json
   "Eb 92/4": {"pdfPages": 441, "parts": [
     {"pdf": "6-140", "content": "Mariages 1760-1787", "pages": "312-178", "note": "Pagination ancienne, décroissante, celle du répertoire ; l'inventaire indique pp. 1-135."},
     {"pdf": "142-201", "content": "Répertoire des mariages 1760-1787"}
   ]},
   "Ed 92/6": {"pdfPages": 516, "parts": [
     {"pdf": "6-456", "content": "Décès 1821-1854", "pages": "1-375", "anchors": [[100, 130], [150, 190], [200, 250]], "note": "Feuillets blancs intercalés tout au long du registre."}
   ]}
   ```
   - `pages` is the pagination the répertoire cites, read on the page; a descending range is fine. When it differs from the inventory's `pp.`, say so in `note`. When the PDF shows the inventory is wrong (swapped parts, wrong pages or dates), also add it to `data/manual/erreurs-acv.md`, and a correction to `data/manual/corrections-inventaire.json` when the fix is certain.
   - Without `anchors`, a part has one PDF page per register page; `build/pdf_pages.py` rejects a mismatch. Split the part where the offset changes: a page number written twice (Eb 92/1 p. 166) goes in both parts, so the site offers both PDF pages.
   - `anchors` are `[page, pdf]` pairs read on the images, for registers whose offset drifts every few pages (blank leaves bound between gatherings, common in Ed). The site interpolates between them and shows « ≈ PDF p. N ». Read one every 50 PDF pages where only blank leaves drift the offset, one every 15-20 where unused forms or bis pages are bound in (all Leysin and Lignerolle Ed volumes), and read one page halfway between two anchors per 100 PDF pages; Ed 15/3 was 5 pages off with a 100-page gap. When the check calls an anchor « incompatible », the page was misread or the pagination skips numbers there (Ed 78/1 pp. 10-11 and 148): read it again, then bisect and split the part. Leave out anchors on the part's first and last pages: its range already gives them.
   - A part may sit inside the previous part's PDF range when pages carry two numbers (Eb 15/1: mariages pp. 72-89 are also baptêmes pp. 339-356). Put the outer part first.
   - `"spread": true` for parts photographed as double pages (Ed 15/2, Ed 3/2): two register pages per PDF page, positions always approximate between anchors. Read anchors on the right-hand page: the site rounds a half page up, so a left-hand anchor sends the right-hand page beside it to the next PDF page. The part may start or end on a right-hand page; one starting on a left-hand page needs an anchor on the right-hand page of the same image (Eb 23/8: `[[2, 5], …]`).
   - A page bound with its transcription before it (Eb 3/1 pp. 1-23, « Traduction de la page ci-contre ») takes two PDF pages: anchors two PDF pages apart on the originals give exact positions.
   - A page carrying several numbers (Eb 10/1 PDF 229: « 218. 219. … 224. » written side by side) is a part of that one PDF page with the whole range: `{"pdf": "229", "pages": "218-224"}`. Every number in it leads to that page.
   - `"roman": true` for a pagination in roman numerals: write `pages` in arabic numbers; the site shows and accepts roman numerals. A pagination that turns roman midway (Eb 118/2 mariages from p. 88) is split there, the roman part flagged.
   - A number that serves two pages, n on the left and « n bis » on the right (Eb 118/1, both parts): `pages` spans the numbers, and anchors two PDF pages apart give exact positions to n; n bis is the next PDF page. End the part on the last n, not its n bis, and put subdivisions such as « 39 ter … 39 X » in a part without `pages`.
   - A part without `pages` (Eb 15/3 décès I-III) still shows its PDF range and highlights for its events.
   - Name events in `content` (« Répertoire des décès », « Baptêmes ») so the site highlights the parts matching the searched event.
   - `pdfPages` equals the profile's `pages`; a test checks it.
6. **Check.** `scripts/check.sh`. Add a case to `pdfCases` in `tests/site_smoke.mjs` for a register with a new kind of layout. A page read as an anchor is exact (« 49 », not « ≈49 »), and a number that falls in several parts of the register returns each of their PDF pages (Eb 118/1 p. 21 is in the baptêmes and the mariages).
7. **Settle or record doubts.** Settle a doubt on the spot when a few page reads answer it: the PDF is open and its pages cached, and a later session pays for them again. Record only what needs more, such as many pages or JPEG 2000 images, in `data/manual/pdf-pages-a-verifier.md`, one row per reading not made on the image: a page number guessed from its neighbours, a part start taken from the inventory, a date read only in part, a stretch between ends or anchors not sampled as above. Give the PDF pages that settle it, so a later pass reads only those. That pass reads the pages, fixes the entry when needed, runs the check and removes the row.

## What the PDFs look like

- **Eb/Ed** (ABBYY Recognition Server, 2014-2015): page 1 is a strip, then covers and a title; register page 1 is usually PDF 5-10. One image per page.
- **Separate répertoires** (1917-1920 for Eb) are bound apart but scanned into the same PDF, right after the part they index, each behind a strip. One PDF may hold the separate répertoires of several registers (Eb 54/1 holds three, covering Eb 54/1-4); their covers list where each section starts (« Mariages … dès page 20 »), so split them into one part per section. **Incorporated répertoires** sit at the end of the volume, usually after a marbled cover, or inside the tête-bêche part.
- **Tête-bêche**: the PDF follows the operator. Usually the main part first, then the flipped volume (a back cover shows) from the tête-bêche start; a tête-bêche répertoire may come right after the part it indexes. Eb 23/8's and Eb 68/2's tête-bêche parts were photographed upside down, from the last page back: `pages` descending, `rotate: true` in `pdf.view` reads them (`from` then counts from the top of the turned page), and for Eb 23/8 the middle image holds the last page of both parts, so one of them gets a nested one-page part.
- **Paginations**: Eb répertoires often cite an older, descending pagination written on the pages (Eb 92/1, 92/2, 92/4, 92/6, 92/7), not the inventory's `pp.`. Many Eb pages carry two numbers, the older one crossed out; when they diverge, read one répertoire line to see which one it cites (Eb 15/9's répertoire cites the newer one, with « anc. pag. » in brackets). The older pagination may run on from the tête-bêche end: Eb 54/1's tête-bêche part holds pp. 2-36 and its baptêmes run from 115 down to 41. Two paginations in different inks may drift apart (Eb 78/7, red and black, 2 pages apart at the end): follow the inventory's and check one répertoire line. Early pages may carry a number on the recto only, the versos blank of numbers (Eb 78/2 up to p. 47): anchors on the numbered rectos. Ed registers have one printed pagination.
- **Irregular Eb pages** (Bex, Aigle): bis, A/B and duplicate numbers, loose slips photographed recto and verso, the same page photographed twice. Each one shifts the offset: split the part there and say what sits in between in `note`.
- **Skipped or corrected numbers** (Aigle): a pagination can skip numbers (Eb 3/3 pp. 284 and 305, Ed 3/10 p. 203, Ed 3/13 pp. 434-443) or be corrected in ink over a stretch (Ed 3/8: 195-202 written over as 198-205). The offset then decreases: an anchor check failing with « incompatible » on a correctly read page means a skip, so bisect and split the part. Use the corrected numbers.
- **Inventory labels**: the inventory can attach a part's page range to the wrong content (Eb 3/6 swaps its two parts): read the title page of each part.
- **Blank leaves**: Ed registers often have unnumbered blank leaves bound in every few pages. Their JPEGs are smaller (about 60 % of the median), a hint for where to look, not proof (no use on L'Étivaz's grayscale Ed scans, but on Ed 68 bis/1 each dip was one blank form). Sizes cost no request once the PDF is open: `await pdf.small(path)` lists the pages under half the median size, `await pdf.sizes(path, first, last)` gives `[page, KB]` pairs. An image of 60-100 KB there is an inserted white sheet numbered « 170 b ».
- **Unused printed forms** (Ballaigues Ed 10/1, 10/3, 10/4): Ed volumes bound with spare blank or crossed-out forms, their « Page ___ » left empty or written « 0 », the offset growing by 20-30 over the first 150 pages. Anchor the stretch, then split exact parts where the offset settles. Ed 10/4 also skips numbers (pp. 139-140, 152-153).
- **Restarted numbering** (Ed 68/4): the numbers go back from 37 to 28 at PDF 45, so pp. 28-37 exist twice: one part per series, and the site offers both PDF pages. Pages past p. 40 carry no number there: they go in a part without `pages`. Ed 68 bis/5 skips n° 86, then has 103 bis and 104 bis. Ed 118/4 goes back from 168 to 160 at PDF 194 (a pencil correction, 170, 172…, covers part of the second series), then skips from 208 to 269.
- **Year tabs and word pagination** (Ormont-Dessous Ed 96/1-8): the profile's many mid-volume `strips` are year tabs, and the page before each tab is usually photographed twice; Ed 96/1 also has a pair of blank leaves (two ~900 KB images) every ~20 pages. Predicting the numbers from image sizes and checking the prediction every 20-30 PDF pages found these quickly; read anchors where it fails. Early pages are numbered in words (« Page cinquante-trois »), and a new year's pages may first restart at « une », then be corrected to follow on.
- **Scanning batches** (Lutry Ed 78/2, 78/3): a mid-volume strip ends a batch, and the last page or two before it are photographed again (Ed 78/3: pp. 294-295 three times over PDF 305-309). Read the pages on both sides of every strip and split the part there.
- **Slips photographed on and off the page** (Cheseaux Eb 25/4, 25/6-9, 25/12): a slip shows as the page with the slip on it, then the slip alone, sometimes the page again without it, and a page is often photographed twice before a strip. Each one shifts the offset by 1-2, every 10-30 pages in Eb 25/9. `pdf.sizes` flags the slip-alone images (under 75 % of the median), not the page-with-slip ones: read the two pages before each flagged image. Two pages showing the same top row are the same page: Eb 25/4 PDF 65 and 66 are both p. 55.
- **A parish's répertoires in one PDF** (Cheseaux): Eb 25/1's PDF holds the separate répertoires of baptêmes, mariages (split hommes / épouses) and décès for all Eb 25 registers, each cover listing the répertoire pages of every register; its own register is photographed in two batches, before and after them. Annex registers (Sullens, Bournens, Boussens) have their catéchumènes, paginated in the middle of the baptêmes, photographed after the baptêmes.
- **JPEG 2000 pages** (Ed 10/1, whose profile is skipped too): `pdf.view` fails with « The source image cannot be decoded ». Return the bytes of `await pdf.imageOf(path, n)` as base64, padded like `exportProfiles` so the tool writes them to a file, about 6 pages per call; then decode, crop and stack the bands with Pillow (`uv run --with pillow`) and read the PNG. Each page costs about 2 requests.
- **Pencil pagination at the foot** (Eb 23/13-15, Eb 78/3's catéchumènes after p. 170, Eb 78/7's tête-bêche p. 100, Eb 25/1's leaves 0a-0c, Eb 25/10's cover p. 37, Ed 78/3 p. 380): no number at the top; read the bottom with `from: 0.9` before calling a page unnumbered.
- **16th-century fragments** (Eb 23/1-3): leaves torn or of uneven size, often mounted on paper, so the number of the next leaf shows above the page's own (Eb 23/2: « 73 » above « 71 »). Trust a number only when the neighbouring pages agree with it.
- **SB 267** (img2pdf, 2022): page objects are spread through the file, so the profile only counts pages; offset +3 (barcode sheet, title, verso); no répertoire in the PDF, which ends at the last act.
- `#page=N` links work in the user's browser (checked on Eb 92/7). The in-app pane cannot display PDFs, and navigating it to a PDF URL opens a download dialog for the user: read pages through `pdf.view`.
- Ollon's 17 Eb/Ed registers took about 370 requests in all, most of them page images. Bex's 24 took about 830: 130 for the profiles, about 45 per Eb register (bisecting the offset shifts) and 15 per Ed register. Aigle's 21 took about 880 in all. Ballaigues's 8 took about 560: the Eb 10/1 baptêmes alone about 120 (slips, skipped numbers, multi-number pages). Château-d'Oex's 26 took about 690, 95 of them for the profiles. L'Étivaz's 8 took about 425, 21 of them for the profiles. Gryon's 8 and Huémoz's 5 took about 730, 46 of them for the profiles. Leysin's 8 and Lignerolle's 8 took about 710, 54 of them for the profiles; their Ed volumes are bound with many unused forms and bis pages, so anchors every 10-20 PDF pages. Ormont-Dessous's 18 took about 690, 62 of them for the profiles. Rougemont's 21 took about 1120, 85 of them for the profiles: Ed 118/1, 118/2, 118/4 and 118/6 have slips photographed on and off the page, graph-paper sheets numbered « 146 b » and crossed-out forms throughout, so anchors every 10 PDF pages; image sizes only flag the graph sheets. Lutry's Eb 78/1-7 and Ed 78/1, 78/8-14 took about 725, 90 of them for the profiles; Ed 78/1 is grayscale with blank leaves and unused forms throughout (offset 3 to 37), and the annonces volumes Ed 78/8-14 each have one bis page, skipped or repeated numbers, or an unnumbered page: bisect any offset change. Lutry's Ed 78/2-7, profiles already saved, took about 470 more: Ed 78/2, 78/4 and 78/6 are bound with blank leaves and unused forms all along (offset up to 57, 82 and 78), read at a stride of 10 PDF pages with `numbers()` and kept as anchors; Ed 78/3 and 78/7 drift only at photographed-twice pages; Ed 78/5 is regular double pages. Cheseaux's 14 Eb registers took about 910 requests, 62 of them for the 19 profiles: slips throughout the annex registers and Eb 25/9, bisected at every offset change; Cheseaux's Ed 25/1-5, profiles already saved, took about 500 more, 75 of them to settle the doubts: Ed 25/1 has a blank leaf, often after an unused form, every 8-20 pages, which `pdf.sizes` flags as pairs of small images, so reading the page after each pair found five numbers used twice (split there) and two bis pages; Ed 25/3's early repeated numbers are « N.B. » pages, notes on an act numbered like the act's page; it skips 77-78, 182-184 and 263-266, and PDF 220-238 also carry an older « du Registre » number that its répertoire cites for 1847 (nested parts give both); Ed 25/4 reads at `from: 0.085` and jumps from 129 to 230, which its répertoire cites; Ed 25/5's répertoire cites the annonces' numbers, not pages.
